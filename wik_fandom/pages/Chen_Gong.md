## Appearance

Gu Can's mother, a close friend of his wife, recalls that he was "far from a handsome man", and that she never thought he was good enough for his wife. *(Chapter 17)*

## Personality

Chen Ping'an's father was quiet, withdrawn and awkward with words, but very good to his son. Whenever he returned to town, he brought the boy small gifts such as candy Bodhisattva figures or old porcelain fragments. Ma Kuxuan remembers him as a famous potter who was extremely good at his craft and a very honest person. *(Chapter 56)* He and his wife were known as generous and kind people.

One of Chen Ping'an's few clear childhood memories is of his father saying perhaps the only romantic thing he ever said in his life. As his wife mended clothes with their son on her lap, he asked her whether they would be together again in their next lives. She smiled and answered, "Why not?" *(Chapter 218 (2))*

## Background

Chen Ping'an's father belonged to the Chen Clan, which had a long and once-prosperous history. Through two periods of upheaval, the clan fell into decline on Eastern Treasured Vial Continent. By the time he was born, it had been in decay for over a thousand years, as if cursed never to rise again. *(Chapter 22)*

He grew up in the small town and worked as a potter. Gu Can's mother believed that, had he not died young, he might have become head potter of a large dragon kiln within twenty years.

Before his son turned six, he shattered Chen Ping'an's [bonded porcelain](Natal_Porcelain.md), effectively tearing up the contract tied to the boy's fate. This stripped Chen Ping'an of his fated opportunities in Jewel Small World, but it also left him free, "like a flickering candle in a dark room". *(Chapter 63 (2))* Ruan Qiong later wonders how an ordinary town potter could have known the secrets of bonded porcelain, and why he would smash his son's porcelain at the cost of his own life. He concludes that someone must have deliberately revealed the secrets to him and encouraged him. Old Man Yang admits he first took it for an ordinary clan conflict, and only realised too late that it was "the true kill shot" of a vast plot targeting Qi Jingchun. *(Chapter 85 (1))*

He died suddenly, and his body was never found. Some townsfolk said he slipped into the creek one rainy day while rushing to protect the kiln flames, while others said he trespassed onto a mountain sealed by the imperial court and was killed by wild beasts. *(Chapter 17)* Ma Kuxuan recalls that on a stormy night around that time, he saw his own father return home secretly with a strange expression of delight and fear, while his mother could not stop smiling. *(Chapter 56)* It is later revealed that Ma Kuxuan's parents were deeply involved in the shattering of Chen Ping'an's bonded porcelain. The porcelain had been bought by an unknown buyer, and the Great Li Empire paid that buyer compensation for its destruction. *(Chapter 530 (2))*

After his death, his grieving wife fell ill and died within a year, leaving Chen Ping'an an orphan.

## Trivia

- Avenging his parents is one of Chen Ping'an's very few lifelong fixations. *(Chapter 364 (1))*
- The elderly Aunty Chen grumbles that being a good person did him no good, since his death brought years of hardship to his wife and son. She tells Chen Ping'an not to resent him, but to treat it as a debt from a past life, repaid in this one. *(Chapter 466 (1))*
- Following an old potters' tradition, Chen Ping'an adds handfuls of soil from his travels to his parents' graves. *(Chapter 158 (2))*
- His son was born on the fifth day of the fifth month, which the town's elders considered the unluckiest day of the year.
