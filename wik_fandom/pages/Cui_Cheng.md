**Cui Cheng** is the grandfather of the Great Li Empire's imperial preceptor [Cui Chan](Cui_Chan.md), a former leader of the Cui Clan, and once one of the most renowned scholars of [Eastern Treasured Vial Continent](Eastern_Treasured_Vial_Continent.md). He became the first pure martial artist from the continent to reach the peak of the tenth tier, and came within a hair of the eleventh. After falling from his peak, he wandered for nearly a hundred years as a barefoot madman, until he came to the small town looking for his grandson's teacher.

He becomes [Chen Ping'an](Chen_Ping_An.md)'s martial arts master, and later [Pei Qian](Pei_Qian.md)'s. His training methods are brutal: he pounds his students' bodies and spirits into shape with his own fists. He makes Chen Ping'an the "strongest third-tier martial artist under the heavens". Beneath his savagery lies a deep and unspoken care for his students.

## Appearance

Cui Cheng first appears as a barefoot, disheveled old man covered in dirt and grass, with murky eyes. On Downtrodden Mountain he usually wears coarse clothes and goes barefoot. After [Lu Chen](Lu_Chen.md)'s visit, and again in his final days, he dresses in a scholar's Confucian robes and boots.

## Personality

Cui Cheng is overbearing, proud and frighteningly blunt. He insults his students mercilessly, calling them trash and cowards, and believes a martial artist must contend with heaven and earth while enduring the most unimaginable pain. He considers the Martial Dao one of the Great Daos and resents how Qi refiners look down on it. He wants every martial artist in the world to feel that his punch is the very heavens.

Yet he is also principled and perceptive. He refuses to interfere with a student without their master's consent, values sincerity, and quietly wipes the blood from Chen Ping'an's face once he is unconscious. He was deeply wounded by his clan's descendants, whom he regards as people who fawned on the powerful. In his last years he grows gentler, eating with the others, drinking with the little boy in azure, and guiding Pei Qian on a final journey.

## Background

For a thousand years, the Cui Clan has been one of the most respected scholarly clans of Eastern Treasured Vial Continent. In his youth, Cui Cheng was one of its top scholars and personally oversaw the young Cui Chan's early studies. He later turned to the Martial Dao and reached the pinnacle of the tenth tier about a century before the story, just missing the eleventh tier, the true peak of martial arts.<sup>[[1]](#ref-1)</sup> He once travelled alone across the Middle Earth Divine Continent, where he suffered a horrific fate, yet was still regarded as a true hero by its greatest martial artists. One of his lifelong regrets was never fighting that continent's martial goddess.

Something involving his grandson Cui Chan caused him to fall from his peak "into a deep abyss" and lose all goodwill towards his clan.<sup>[[2]](#ref-2)</sup> For nearly a hundred years he was a pitiful madman, only occasionally lucid.

## Plot

### Searching for Cui Chan's Teacher

Cui Chan, now the Great Li Empire's imperial preceptor, blocks his grandfather on a street near the small town. The half-mad old man does not recognise him and pushes past, insisting he must find his grandson's teacher.<sup>[[3]](#ref-3)</sup> He finds Chen Ping'an at Downtrodden Mountain, introduces himself as Cui Chan's grandfather, anxiously asks whether his grandson is being bullied, and moves into the bamboo building. He declares the building a fine place where he can stay clear-headed, and offers to help Chen Ping'an.<sup>[[4]](#ref-4)</sup> It is later revealed that the talismanic characters [Li Xisheng](Li_Xisheng.md) wrote on the building are what freed him from his madness.<sup>[[5]](#ref-5)</sup>

When Cui Chan visits, Cui Cheng says that this is not the "Chanchan" he remembers, observing that his grandson used to always add a kind word after speaking ill of anyone.<sup>[[6]](#ref-6)</sup> When Sun Shujian, a guest elder of the Cui Clan at the peak of the sixth tier, asks to learn from him, Cui Cheng kills him with a single punch thrown at only fifth-tier strength, calling him unworthy.<sup>[[7]](#ref-7)</sup>

### Training Chen Ping'an

Cui Cheng then begins Chen Ping'an's training, an ordeal more painful than death. He orders him to flay himself and sever his own tendons, beats him unconscious and awake again, and hammers him with his signature Deity Drumming Technique, once rewarding him with 31 punches instead of 30. He also teaches the Rain Evaporation Technique and the Heavy Cavalry Formation Shattering Technique. Even Wei Bo knocks on the door to tell him not to go too far, and is snapped at that few people have the right to judge his teaching. Chen Ping'an curses him in return, and Cui Cheng remarks fondly that the boy reminds him of the young Cui Chan.

Through this training, Chen Ping'an becomes the strongest third-tier martial artist under the heavens. Cui Cheng offers to temper his fourth tier as well, but Chen Ping'an refuses and leaves to deliver Ning Yao's sword. Cui Cheng calls him a coward for it.<sup>[[8]](#ref-8)</sup> Wei Bo later realises that had Chen Ping'an accepted, the old man would have died without regrets, with catastrophic consequences for the small town.

When Lu Chen visits Downtrodden Mountain, Cui Cheng bows to him first as a Cui Clan scholar and then as a martial artist, with fierce fighting spirit.<sup>[[9]](#ref-9)</sup> Although he has returned to the peak of the tenth tier, he ultimately does not throw a punch at Lu Chen.<sup>[[10]](#ref-10)</sup> While Chen Ping'an travels, Cui Cheng remains in the bamboo building, looking after the place and the two children. Chen Ping'an later recalls his teachings constantly, including his dismissal of the *Mountain Shaking Guide* as crude.

### Second Round of Training

When Chen Ping'an returns from Bamboo Scroll Lake, Cui Cheng beats him unconscious on sight for having grown less alert, and makes him sleep. He scolds him for letting Pei Qian waste her talent and for his clumsiness with Ruan Xiu. Before their next training session, he finally introduces himself as Cui Cheng, former leader of the Cui Clan, and reveals that Cui Minghuang of Lake View Academy belongs to a branch of the clan.<sup>[[11]](#ref-11)</sup>

He brings Chen Ping'an's fist intent "to life" through even harsher sessions. He also makes [Zhu Lian](Zhu_Lian.md) spar with Chen Ping'an under his supervision, threatening to kill Zhu Lian if he holds back, only for Zhu Lian to start deliberately provoking him to earn beatings of his own. Privately, Cui Cheng muses that fearing death is good for the young, and that Chen Ping'an should become strong enough to fight the martial goddess of the Middle Earth Divine Continent in his place.<sup>[[12]](#ref-12)</sup> When Chen Ping'an breaks through to the fifth tier, Cui Cheng dismisses it as a minor matter while struggling to hide his smile.

### Pei Qian's Master

While Chen Ping'an is away, Pei Qian comes to the second floor for the first time and asks to learn from him. Cui Cheng accepts her as his direct disciple and immediately smashes her into the wall.<sup>[[13]](#ref-13)</sup> He trains her as brutally as he did her master, insulting both of them until her anger finally becomes fist intent strong enough to make him dodge.

## Abilities

- **Tenth tier martial artist:** at the pinnacle of the tenth tier, one of only three End Tier martial artists on Eastern Treasured Vial Continent, alongside Li Er and Song Changjing.
- **Deity Drumming Technique**: his proudest fist technique, which strikes repeatedly once the first punch lands. It tempers both the body and the soul, and Li Er considers it one of the best fist techniques in the world.
- **Rain Evaporation Technique** and **Heavy Cavalry Formation Shattering Technique**: both taught to Chen Ping'an.
- **Teaching:** a "sagacious teacher" able to tailor punishment to drive impurities from a student's body at each tier. Li Er notes that only Cui Cheng could have laid Chen Ping'an's foundation.
- **Scholarship:** an accomplished Confucian scholar and calligraphy connoisseur.

## Trivia

- Cui Cheng judged a martial artist's aptitude by whether they could draw profound fist intent from the lowliest of techniques, which was one reason he chose Chen Ping'an.
- He calls his grandson "Chanchan".
- During their first training sessions, many of the insults Cui Cheng hurled at Chen Ping'an had actually been picked up from Chen Ping'an's own trash talk.
