**Old Man Yao** was the master potter who ran the Treasure Creek dragon kiln outside the [Small Town](Small_Town.md). He took in [Chen Ping'an](Chen_Ping_An.md) as an apprentice and made [Liu Xianyang](Liu_Xianyang.md) his favourite disciple. He had already died when the story opens, but his lessons and sayings stay with Chen Ping'an long after the kilns close.

## Appearance

He was an old man who liked to keep to himself, often standing with his hands tucked into the opposite sleeves. Out in the mountains he seemed to come alive, climbing with more energy than a healthy young man. His hair and eye colour are never described.

## Personality

Old Man Yao was gruff, short-tempered and sparing with his words. When he did speak, it was usually to curse someone, and his remarks were either cutting or so cryptic that nobody could make sense of them. He beat and insulted his apprentices freely. Even Liu Xianyang, whom he plainly favoured, was not spared. Yet anyone could see that he meant well. After he once split Liu Xianyang's forehead open, he paced his room most of the night and finally sent Chen Ping'an over with a bottle of ointment.<sup>[[1]](#ref-1)</sup>

He was harder on Chen Ping'an than on anyone. Thinking the boy had no talent, he refused to teach him the real secrets of pottery and set him to manual labour instead. Once, disgusted with him, he left the boy alone in the wilderness and walked back to the kiln. Chen Ping'an had to find his own way home through about 60 kilometres of mountains in the pouring rain.<sup>[[2]](#ref-2)</sup> Still, Old Man Yao trusted him with the kiln's money for buying medicine, because he knew the boy was honest.

He took his craft very seriously. A single firing could waste half a year of work if it went wrong, so he insisted on watching the fire himself and sat up through whole nights on his stool beside the kiln.<sup>[[3]](#ref-3)</sup> When a worker let the kiln fire go out and fled into the mountains, Old Man Yao sent men after him and broke his arms and legs when he was dragged back.<sup>[[4]](#ref-4)</sup> He could be kind in quieter ways too. While other kilns allowed three days off at harvest time, he always gave four or five.<sup>[[5]](#ref-5)</sup>

When he drank, he became a different man. He would boast that their kiln made porcelain for the emperor himself, and he told strange stories about gods in the mountains and rivers that his apprentices loved to hear.<sup>[[6]](#ref-6)</sup>

## Background

### The kiln

Old Man Yao was the most skilled potter among the town's dragon kilns. As a young man he was the only potter in dozens of kilns who could make a difficult kind of underglaze porcelain favoured by an earlier Great Li emperor.<sup>[[7]](#ref-7)</sup> His jumping burin technique won praise from everyone who saw it. He took several disciples, but none of them truly met his standards.

Liu Xianyang became his best disciple and learned in half a year what took Chen Ping'an three years.<sup>[[8]](#ref-8)</sup> Old Man Yao clearly preferred him and expected him to take over the kiln someday. That favour had limits. When he overheard Liu Xianyang calling himself a prodigy, he flew into a rage and beat him, and Liu Xianyang never used the word again.<sup>[[9]](#ref-9)</sup>

### Chen Ping'an

Liu Xianyang brought the orphaned Chen Ping'an to the kiln, and Old Man Yao took the boy in without ever accepting him as a true disciple. Instead he made Chen Ping'an carry his tools on long trips into the mountains to search for clay. Along the way he taught the boy to judge soil by chewing it, until Chen Ping'an could tell a kiln and its master from a broken shard just by holding it. On one trip they reached a high peak and saw an even taller mountain to the east. In a rare talkative mood, Old Man Yao told him to look but not to talk about it afterwards, because a person should keep his head down and work.<sup>[[10]](#ref-10)</sup>

On their last trip into the mountains, he pointed out the burial ground of the other branch of the Chen Clan. He also told Chen Ping'an that not even immortals could find where his own branch's ancestors lay.<sup>[[11]](#ref-11)</sup> Chen Ping'an never held his treatment against him and remained grateful to him all his life. He later calls Old Man Yao his "half-teacher," and one of his sayings became a guiding principle for Chen Ping'an: if something is meant to be yours, hold on to it and don't let go, and if it isn't, don't even think about it.<sup>[[12]](#ref-12)</sup>

According to Song Jixin's mother, Old Man Yao also told the young Chen Ping'an the truth about the superstition surrounding his birth on the fifth day of the fifth month. The boy ran back from the kiln in a fury, cornered Song Jixin outside Clay Vase Alley and nearly strangled him. She blames Old Man Yao for the fear Song Jixin carried for years afterwards.<sup>[[13]](#ref-13)</sup>

### Death and legacy

Old Man Yao died some time before the story begins, and the dragon kilns were shut down afterwards. 

When [Qi Jingchun](Qi_Jingchun.md) asked the town's old locust tree for a blessed leaf to protect Chen Ping'an, a single leaf fell bearing the golden character "Yao." Qi Jingchun confirmed it was the same Yao as the boy's old master and asked whether he would repay the Yao Clan's kindness. Chen Ping'an vowed to save any member of the Yao Clan connected to him, even at the cost of his life.<sup>[[14]](#ref-14)</sup> He later used that leaf to keep Liu Xianyang alive.

Years later, Cui Chan and Cui Dongshan review the scenes of Chen Ping'an's past that involve Old Man Yao and study the fall of that leaf closely. Cui Dongshan concludes that Old Man Yao had hidden a plan of his own extremely well, and that [Old Man Yang](Old_Man_Yang.md) must have been part of it.<sup>[[15]](#ref-15)</sup>

## Trivia

- Old Man Yao believed that all mountains, tall or short, grow from the same roots and are related to each other like members of a clan.
- He taught that every kind of clay has its own fate, much like people do.
- He called True Jewel Mountain's soil the most flavourful he knew, but said the mountain was too small, like a man trapped in a cage.
- He passed on an old potters' custom that Chen Ping'an kept up for years, though he never explained what it meant.
- When the kiln supervision official visited in his robes, Old Man Yao kept the other apprentices out of sight and had only Liu Xianyang show off the jumping burin technique.
- [Old Man Yang](Old_Man_Yang.md) reacts with an odd expression whenever Old Man Yao is mentioned.
- Even [Pei Qian](Pei_Qian.md), watching scenes from her master's childhood years later, wonders why Old Man Yao was always scolding him.
