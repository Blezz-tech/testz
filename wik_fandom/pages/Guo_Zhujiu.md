## Appearance

As a child Guo Zhujiu was known for running around wrapped in nothing but a bedsheet and climbing in and out of houses through windows and over roofs rather than using doors, a habit she kept up until Dong Bude took her in hand and disciplined her out of it.

## Personality

She is headstrong and relentless once she has decided on something, shown by how long and how persistently she pestered Chen Ping’an to accept her as a disciple even after he turned her down.<sup>[[1]](#ref-1)</sup> Underneath the mischief she is also generous and a bit of a schemer for a good cause, as when she talked the local children into buying wine to help Chen Ping’an and Die Zhang’s new shop get started, and left a quiet, affectionate note carved into a wooden tablet there rather than announcing the gesture outright.<sup>[[2]](#ref-2)</sup> She also has real nerve and a protective streak, willing to fight off armed attackers bare-handed to save a stranger’s life.<sup>[[3]](#ref-3)</sup>

## Background

Guo Zhujiu is the daughter of Sword Immortal Guo Jia of the Guo Clan, a first-rate clan at the Sword Qi Great Wall, and was seen from a young age as having an innate talent for the sword and a likely future pillar of her clan. As a child she was unruly, until Dong Bude stepped in to discipline her.

She latched onto the idea of becoming Chen Ping’an’s disciple and refused to let it go despite his repeated refusals. Chen Ping’an uses a naming-based method to judge whether someone is suited to be his disciple, and he initially felt her name, meaning “bamboo wine,” was not suitable, so he told her to wait three years instead of accepting her outright. Around this time others took to calling her “Lü Duan.”<sup>[[1]](#ref-1)</sup>

She helped Chen Ping’an and Die Zhang when they opened a new wine shop, getting the neighborhood children to come buy wine to help the business get on its feet, and left a secret, affectionate inscription on a wooden tablet in the shop without taking credit for it.<sup>[[2]](#ref-2)</sup>

The turning point in her relationship with Chen Ping’an came when she stepped in to rescue an emaciated boy being set upon by assassins in an alley, killing one attacker with her bare hands before being struck across the forehead by a flying sword, leaving a deep gash. The attack turned out to be a targeted assassination attempt on her specifically, rooted in rising political tension between the Ning Clan and the Guo Clan, and echoing an earlier, separate assassination attempt made on Ning Yao at the Mirage Market. Her father Guo Jia arrived afterward and negotiated her punishment down to roughly a year of being grounded. It also came out that Chen Ping’an had quietly arranged for Nalan Yexing to shadow and protect her whenever she went out, showing that he had already unofficially treated her as his disciple well before making it official. After this incident he decided to formally take her on as his disciple without further delay.<sup>[[3]](#ref-3)</sup>

She met Pei Qian for the first time shortly after, addressing her as senior sister.<sup>[[4]](#ref-4)</sup>

She was later formally entered into Downtrodden Mountain’s genealogical records as Chen Ping’an’s disciple. Despite her natural talent for the sword, she chose to train in fist techniques instead. Her cultivation was confirmed at the Sea Observation Tier, and she received a treasure-storage bracelet as a gift from Chen Ping’an, delivered through Cui Dongshan. Pei Qian jokingly referred to her afterward as their master’s “pseudo-closing disciple,” though the actual title of closing disciple belongs to Chen Ping’an himself under the Scholarly Sage, not to her.<sup>[[5]](#ref-5)</sup>

## Trivia

Her name, Zhujiu, means “bamboo wine,” which is why Chen Ping’an initially felt it was unsuitable for a disciple under his naming method.
She is nicknamed “Lü Duan” by others.
She chose to learn fist techniques rather than sword techniques despite being recognized as having natural sword talent.
