# Large Worlds

[← Wiki Index](../INDEX.md) · [Original Fandom Page](https://sword-of-coming.fandom.com/wiki/Large_Worlds)

---
Large worlds are a fundamental part of the cosmology of the narrative, representing the most significant and powerful realms in existence.

**Definition and Nature:**

- Large worlds are described as realms that **run through heaven and earth** and possess an **abundance of spiritual energy**.
- They are considered **bona fide immortal abodes**, making them ideal places for cultivation.
- They are distinct from the 36 small worlds and are much more expansive.
- The rulers of these worlds are **blessed with immense fortune**, and these worlds have already been divided among the most powerful figures of the three teachings.

**Number and Organization:**

- There are **10 large worlds** in total.
- These worlds are the **most significant realms in the cosmology**, and they are usually controlled by powerful figures or groups.
- They are often contrasted with the smaller, less stable mystical realms.
- The ten large worlds are often referenced as a group when compared to the thirty-six small worlds.

**Significance and Power:**

- Cultivating in large worlds can significantly **speed up a Qi refiner's progress** due to the abundant spiritual energy.
- The **most powerful figures in the three teachings** have already divided the large worlds among themselves, making it difficult for others to gain control over them.
- Large worlds are seen as the **ultimate destinations for cultivators** seeking to reach the highest levels of power and enlightenment.
- They are **the source of power for many major forces** and are often the heart of the most important events and conflicts.

**Comparison with Small Worlds:**

- While both large and small worlds can possess significant spiritual energy, large worlds are generally considered to be **more stable and more complete**.
- Small worlds are often described as damaged or incomplete compared to the **pristine nature** of the large worlds.
- Large worlds are **more vast** and hold more spiritual energy than small worlds.
- The ten large worlds are usually the **center of power for major factions**, while the 36 small worlds are often hidden and more independent.

**Examples of Large Worlds**

- The sources do not explicitly name all ten of the large worlds.
- However, one source mentions the **Middle Earth Divine Continent** as being like "the glowing gem that was Middle Earth Divine Continent" and that several major empires exist on that continent.
- It is also mentioned that the **Lotus Flower World** is one of the 36 small worlds, and legend states it is where the Dao Ancestor goes to relax. It is not one of the ten large worlds, despite its size.

**Rulers of Large Worlds**

- The rulers of large worlds are described as people **blessed with immense fortune**, and they belong to the most powerful figures of the three teachings.
- The sources imply that these rulers are some of the **most powerful beings in the cosmos**, and their control over the large worlds is absolute.

**Relationship to Cultivation:**

- Large worlds provide an environment where Qi refiners can significantly **accelerate their cultivation** due to the abundance of spiritual energy.
- The division of the large worlds among the most powerful figures suggests that control over these worlds is key to **attaining immense power and influence**.

The text implies that to become a Sage, one must occupy and control a large world.

The large worlds are **the most powerful and significant realms in the story**. They are the pinnacle of cultivation, offering unparalleled resources and opportunities for those who can access them. They are the focus of much of the power struggle in the narrative and are essential to understanding the cosmology and dynamics of the world. The immense fortune, the vast territory, and the control over these worlds make them central to the ambitions of cultivators and the overall narrative. They are also the benchmark against which all other worlds are compared, highlighting their immense importance in the grand scheme of the cosmos.
