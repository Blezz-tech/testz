# Blessed Lands

[← Wiki Index](../INDEX.md) · [Original Fandom Page](https://sword-of-coming.fandom.com/wiki/Blessed_Lands)

---
Blessed lands are significant locations in the narrative, often described as places of abundant spiritual energy and unique opportunities for cultivation.

**Definition and Nature**:

- Blessed lands are territories with high concentrations of spiritual energy, making them ideal for cultivation. *(Chapter 249)*
- They can arise from various sources, including shattered small worlds,Chapter 37 ancient battlefields, or natural formations.
- Some blessed lands are connected to other small worlds, which makes them unique compared to other blessed lands.
- They are considered independent and are often compared to subsidiary nations.
- The sources also describe blessed lands as being like a "small world" or "mystical realm".
- They are not necessarily places of good fortune, and may be dangerous.

**Formation and Origins**:

- Blessed lands can form when small worlds shatter and fall to earth. *(Chapter 73)*
- They can also be created from ancient ruins or battlefields, which are imbued with spiritual energy.
- Some blessed lands may form naturally due to unique environmental conditions.
- They can be the result of unique geography or the presence of powerful natural elements.

**Types of Blessed Lands**:

- Some blessed lands, like the **Limpid Pond Blessed Land**, are considered lower quality, while others, such as the **Cloud Cave Blessed Land**, are considered high-quality.
- The **Golden Millet Blessed Land** is unique in that it can reflect one's Dao heart, and it was once a bustling world filled with beautiful people and scenes.
- **Lotus Flower Blessed Land** is a medium-grade blessed land known for producing many martial arts prodigies. *(Chapter 319)*
- There are also unique blessed lands that form in the human body.

**Significance for Cultivation**:

- Blessed lands offer a conducive environment for cultivation, as they are brimming with spiritual energy, which is essential for Qi refiners.
- They often contain rare herbs, creatures, and treasures that can greatly enhance cultivation.
- Prodigious figures often come from these blessed lands, and are usually claimed by whichever nation or sect owns the land.
- Some blessed lands are connected to other small worlds, providing unique opportunities for cultivation and growth.
- Cultivating in a blessed land can provide a geographic advantage to a cultivator.
- The quality of a blessed land can greatly influence a cultivator's progress, with higher quality blessed lands providing more resources and opportunities.
- Some blessed lands may have different laws of the Heavenly Dao, which can also impact the resources that are available and how they are used.

**Fated Opportunities**:

- Blessed lands are often associated with fated opportunities, where cultivators may find rare treasures or insights that can lead to rapid advancement.
- These opportunities can vary greatly, from rare items to opportunities for enlightenment.
- It is said that the best fated opportunities are those that can be grabbed, withstood, and digested.
- Some blessed lands are said to be more likely to give fated opportunities to those who are lucky, while others may provide opportunities based on an individual's ability and perseverance.
- Some blessed lands are said to offer insights into the true nature of one's heart and soul.

**Control and Ownership**:

- Blessed lands are often controlled by powerful immortal clans or sects. *(Chapter 129)* *(Chapter 360)*
- Some blessed lands are claimed by nations or empires, who may use them as a source of wealth and influence.
- The control of a blessed land can be a major source of power and wealth.
- Those with control of a blessed land may receive passive benefits without any effort on their part.
- Some blessed lands are open to the public for a fee, while others may be restricted to certain individuals or groups.

**Challenges and Limitations**:

- Entering blessed lands can be dangerous, and not everyone who enters them will obtain a fated opportunity.
- Blessed lands that are open to the public may have a high turnover rate and may be seen as places to spend money for entertainment rather than as places for serious cultivation. *(Chapter 67)*
- Some blessed lands may be damaged or incomplete, which can limit the benefits that they can offer.
- The laws of the Heavenly Dao may differ in blessed lands, which can make them unpredictable and dangerous.
- Some blessed lands may be guarded by powerful entities, making it dangerous for inexperienced cultivators to explore them.
- The flow of time may also differ in blessed lands, which can make it difficult to gauge the amount of time that has passed.

**Examples of Blessed Lands**:

- **Jewel Small World** (now called **Jewel Blessed Land**): A former small world that fell to earth and became a blessed land, though its spiritual energy dropped sharply in the process. *(Chapter 75)*
- **Limpid Pond Blessed Land**: A low-quality blessed land controlled by the Divine Edict Sect.
- **Cloud Cave Blessed Land**: A high-quality blessed land controlled by the Jiang Clan of the Jade Tablet Sect.
- **Golden Millet Blessed Land**: A blessed land that can reflect one's Dao heart. Only its remnants are left now, and drinking its Forgetting Sorrow Wine has become a stop cultivators make a point of visiting. *(Chapter 628)*
- **Lotus Flower Blessed Land**: A medium-grade blessed land that produces many martial arts prodigies.
- **Ascending True Immortal Mystical Realm**: Not itself one of the 72 blessed lands, but a damaged mystical realm derived from one of them, still able to rival Jewel Small World in size. *(Chapter 286)*

Blessed lands are crucial locations in the narrative that offer both opportunities and challenges for cultivators. They are places of intense spiritual energy, rare resources, and unique fated opportunities, but they can also be dangerous and unpredictable. Control over a blessed land is often a major source of power and wealth, making them central to many conflicts and ambitions. Understanding the nature and significance of blessed lands is key to understanding the dynamics of cultivation and the overall story.
