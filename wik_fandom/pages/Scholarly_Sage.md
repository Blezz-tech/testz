The **Scholarly Sage**, usually called **the old scholar**, is a [Confucian](Confucianism.md) sage who was once the fourth most revered sage in the Confucian temples, after the Supreme Sage, the Etiquette Sage and the Second Sage. After losing a secret contest of doctrines known as the "battle between third and fourth", his statues were demoted and finally smashed, his works were banned and burned across the seventy-two academies, and he imprisoned himself in the Forest of Virtue to await death.

He is the teacher of [Cui Chan](Cui_Chan.md), [Zuo You](Zuo_You.md) and [Qi Jingchun](Qi_Jingchun.md). After Qi Jingchun's death, he acknowledges [Chen Ping'an](Chen_Ping_An.md) as his final, "closing" disciple. Though he looks like a short, shabby, wine-loving old man who would rather argue than fight, he is one of the most formidable figures in the world, and his lineage is famed for producing few disciples who are all exceptional.

## Appearance

The old scholar is short, skinny and white-haired. He usually wears azure robes and dresses like a poor travelling scholar, with a bundle of luggage wrapped in cotton cloth on his back. Chen Ping'an notes that he looks even more shabby and impoverished than the noble scholar Zhong Kui. He likes to drink other people's wine and feigns drunkenness to avoid paying. Only on rare occasions does he look like a towering sage, such as when he stands against the mountain wind on Tassel Mountain.<sup>[[1]](#ref-1)</sup> His portraits in old books show a dignified, magnificent figure, and the people who meet him in person are often surprised.

## Personality

The old scholar is talkative, playful, boastful and openly shameless when it suits him. He is famous for winning arguments. He won a precious token from the old Daoist priest of the Lotus Flower Blessed Land by rolling on the ground and wailing, and he pestered the god of Tassel Mountain for a month to hear him praise his final disciple. When Chen Qingdu asked why he, rather than a more fitting sage, had come to the Sword Qi Great Wall, he answered simply that he was shameless enough.<sup>[[2]](#ref-2)</sup> He is also fiercely protective of his disciples, so much so that others call his whole lineage "unbelievably protective of their own." The old Daoist calls him "the most biased person" he has ever met.

Beneath this, he is a profound thinker. His central idea is the "theory of sequential order", a way of finding the proper order in which to judge any matter. He believes human nature must be educated and guided towards goodness, and that principles do not belong to books alone. He readily admits that Confucian teachings have flaws, and that even a small child can speak reason. Spiritual Master Fire Dragon calls him "unmatched" in his assessment of human nature.

He carries a good deal of guilt. He believes that many of the wrongs that befell his lineage trace back to him, and he regrets having taught his closing disciple so little beyond a few brief sentences and some drunken remarks.<sup>[[3]](#ref-3)</sup>

## Background

The old scholar began life as an impoverished, nameless scholar. He spent forty years passing the imperial examinations, and lived in a dilapidated alley on the [Middle Earth Divine Continent](Middle_Earth_Divine_Continent.md) with his first disciples, Cui Chan, Zuo You and later Qi Jingchun, so poor that they sometimes dug worms to fish for food. A'Liang also spent time with them. The old scholar later wrote his books and rose to become the Scholarly Sage. At his height he defeated the sages of Buddhism and Daoism in debate, and he once went to the border of two worlds to hurl abuse at the Dao Ancestor's second disciple.

He then lost the "battle between third and fourth" with the Second Sage. As agreed, he imprisoned himself in the Forest of Virtue, watching helplessly as his statues were demoted and smashed. His first disciple Cui Chan betrayed him, and his lineage fell into disarray. When his favourite disciple, Qi Jingchun, died challenging the Heavenly Dao on a distant continent, the old scholar made an unprecedented deal with the sages of every teaching to break his vow. In exchange, he gave up his physical body and his powers as a Confucian sage, and now travels the world as a soul.<sup>[[4]](#ref-4)</sup>

## Plot

### Meeting Chen Ping'an

The old scholar first appears drifting on a small boat, describing himself to a boatman as "half a teacher" of a certain boy.<sup>[[5]](#ref-5)</sup> He arranges for the young Cui Chan, a split-off half of the imperial preceptor, to become Chen Ping'an's student, and places a restriction on Cui Chan's mind that punishes any ill thought towards Chen Ping'an. Unknown to Cui Chan, Qi Jingchun had already taken Chen Ping'an as a disciple on his teacher's behalf and given him the old scholar's final relic, a jade hairpin.<sup>[[6]](#ref-6)</sup>

When the sword spirit, the tall woman in white bound to Chen Ping'an, wants to kill Cui Chan, the old scholar arrives and tries to talk her out of it. She addresses him as the Scholarly Sage and challenges him. He scolds Cui Chan over his betrayal and over Ma Zhan's death, reminding him that he himself taught Cui Chan Go, and that Qi Jingchun once beat Cui Chan but chose not to tell him.

He then fights the sword spirit and Chen Ping'an inside a picture scroll that is also his own consciousness. He conjures an illusion of Tassel Mountain, one of the Five Mountains of the Middle Earth Divine Continent, and stands on its summit while Chen Ping'an unleashes the sword spirit's strikes. One strike splits the mountain, drawing the anger of its golden-armoured god, an old acquaintance whom the old scholar talks into giving his "final disciple" a gift.

At the Autumn Reed Inn, he discusses the Great Dao with Chen Ping'an, Li Baoping and Cui Chan. He sets out his theory of sequential order, explains why Qi Jingchun spared Cui Chan, draws out the shadow of Chen Ping'an's worries from his body, and asks whether Chen Ping'an is willing to follow Qi Jingchun's arrangement and become his disciple.<sup>[[7]](#ref-7)</sup> He takes Chen Ping'an and Li Baoping to bookshops and a tavern, gets drunk on cheap wine, tells them stories about his life, and makes Chen Ping'an carry him home on his back, slapping his head and insisting that youths must drink. Before leaving, he gives Chen Ping'an a silver ingot that is really a sword billet, which he calls "Little Capital" and Chen Ping'an later renames "First". He gently points out to Li Baoping that, as Qi Jingchun's teacher, he is her grandmaster, and accepts her formal bow.<sup>[[8]](#ref-8)</sup>

Chen Ping'an initially declines to become his disciple, feeling unworthy since he was not even a student. The old scholar takes it in good humour.<sup>[[9]](#ref-9)</sup> Qi Jingchun, appearing to Chen Ping'an later, tells him that he regards him as his junior brother regardless.

### Guarding His Disciple from Afar

At the Sword Qi Great Wall, the old scholar appears before the Chen Clan's Chen Chun'an, spinning a small sun in his palm and asking his opinion of "my final disciple", then answering his own question: very good.

In the Lotus Flower Blessed Land, it is revealed that he once won a token from its master, the old Daoist priest, together with a promise that the Daoist would protect whoever held it. The old scholar then secretly planted that "Go piece", [Pei Qian](Pei_Qian.md), in the blessed land.<sup>[[10]](#ref-10)</sup> He spends a month pestering the god of Tassel Mountain to praise Chen Ping'an.

When the Ascension Tier cultivator [Du Mao](Du_Mao.md) attacks Chen Ping'an and Zheng Dafeng at [Old Dragon City](Old_Dragon_City.md), the old scholar senses it through divination on Tassel Mountain and rushes over. He freezes the city in time, lectures two disciples of the Second Sage on shame and justice, and agrees to clean up the mess in exchange for a golden jade pendant from an almighty figure in the heavens. When that figure lingers, the old scholar threatens to explain how he really lost the battle between third and fourth, then bites the pendant to check that it is real.<sup>[[11]](#ref-11)</sup> He beats a young Daoist priest who had a hand in the trouble with a tree branch, and finds his second disciple Zuo You, who had been nearby, then lets him go to the Sword Qi Great Wall as he had always wanted.

Through the Zhao Clan's yin soul, he passes on the Parasol Leaf Sect's compensation to Chen Ping'an, including Du Mao's Ascension Tier fragments, and gives him copies of two mountain-protection formations, from Peace and Tranquility Mountain and Planchette Writing Sect.

During Chen Ping'an's crisis of the heart at [Bamboo Scroll Lake](Bamboo_Scroll_Lake.md), the old scholar stays on Tassel Mountain day after day watching events, ignoring the Confucian temple's envoys. To the mountain god, he describes his disciples: Cui Chan was the most literary but pessimistic, Qi Jingchun the most knowledgeable and optimistic, and Zuo You the most stubborn but the best-balanced, learning the sword so quickly that the old scholar feared he would become the first fourteenth-tier sword cultivator in thousands of years. He adds that his fourth disciple is only "half a disciple", because the man feels his background is too lowly and does not want to cause his master trouble.<sup>[[12]](#ref-12)</sup>

### The Sword Qi Great Wall

The old scholar later comes to the Sword Qi Great Wall in person. Zuo You bows to him as "Master." He goes to Chen Qingdu's thatched cottage and announces that the scholarly temple needs to borrow three people. Chen Qingdu refuses. The old scholar keeps going back to haggle, at one point declaring he will roll on the ground and shout if he cannot win this time, until the strain nearly makes his insubstantial body vanish.

Afterwards he goes to the wine shop where Chen Ping'an works, eats and drinks happily on a stool with his two disciples, and scolds Zuo You for not treating his junior brother better. For the first time, Chen Ping'an calls him simply "Master." Chen Ping'an explains that he refused before because he had not yet studied, and that he now accepts the Scholarly Sage as his teacher. Before leaving, the old scholar thanks [Ning Yao](Ning_Yao.md), who has no idea why.<sup>[[13]](#ref-13)</sup> He also quietly passes a book Chen Ping'an had given him on to Zuo You.

He escorts Chen Ping'an's sword spirit back to the Majestic World and conceals her aura on the road. Along the way he uses up a store of merit that belongs to Chen Ping'an, explaining that it would be a waste to leave it, and that he would rather spend it before his disciple ever learns it existed.<sup>[[14]](#ref-14)</sup>

### Downtrodden Mountain

When Downtrodden Mountain's ancestral hall is founded, three portraits are hung inside. The old scholar's occupies the centre, flanked by those of Qi Jingchun and Cui Cheng.<sup>[[15]](#ref-15)</sup>

He later pays a secret visit to the hall, his third visit to the mountain.
