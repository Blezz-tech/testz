**Zhao Yao** is the eldest grandson of the Zhao Clan, one of the four great families of the [Small Town](Small_Town.md). He was [Qi Jingchun](Qi_Jingchun.md)'s student and scholarly attendant at the private school, and he left the town carrying his teacher's most important seal.

## Appearance

Zhao Yao is a neat, refined young scholar who usually dresses in azure.<sup>[[1]](#ref-1)</sup> At the Go board he sits with a serious face and his back ramrod straight.<sup>[[2]](#ref-2)</sup> Chen Ping'an counts him among the good-looking people of his own age. 

## Personality

Zhao Yao is gentle, earnest and well mannered, the opposite of [Song Jixin](Song_Jixin.md)'s sharp tongue and pride.<sup>[[3]](#ref-3)</sup> Elders across the town held him up as the model their own children should follow. That left him with few real friends, since the rowdy young masters of Fortune Street thought him a dull bookworm. Song Jixin, his regular Go partner, was about the only person he could call a friend. Zhao Yao admired Song Jixin and always lost to him at Go. Song Jixin privately thought Zhao Yao was not as clever as he was, but stayed friendly with him because of their shared teacher.<sup>[[4]](#ref-4)</sup>

He is diligent and memorised every book Qi Jingchun gave him. He is also prone to embarrassment and blushes easily. The first time he saw [Ning Yao](Ning_Yao.md), he stood frozen in the street, staring at her until his teacher had to pull him away, then blurted out that she smelled really good. Qi Jingchun later told him not to fall for her.<sup>[[5]](#ref-5)</sup>

He has a strong sense of obligation. When a stranger saved his life, he insisted on giving the man his most treasured possession, saying he could not simply ignore such a debt.

## Background

### Fortune Street

Zhao Yao served Qi Jingchun as both student and scholarly attendant, and he spent more time with his teacher than any other child at the private school. Qi Jingchun carved a seal for him that bore the words "the world welcomes spring."<sup>[[6]](#ref-6)</sup> Song Jixin tried to trade for it several times, and Zhao Yao refused every time. Shortly before everything changed in the town, Qi Jingchun walked him past the stone archways and warned him to guard his tongue from then on, telling him that careless words would only bring trouble.

On the night before he left, Zhao Yao brought Song Jixin a pouch of Good Fortune Coins as a farewell gift and drank with him until he could barely stand. When he got home, his grandmother sat him down in the study before a wooden carving of a resting dragon and solemnly made him take the seat behind the desk. The next morning he left the town on a cart, reciting poetry at the top of his voice. He passed Chen Ping'an on the road as he went.<sup>[[7]](#ref-7)</sup>

### The spring seal

Following his grandfather's plans, Zhao Yao set out by ox cart for an immortal sect in the west of the Eastern Treasured Vial Continent. Halfway there he met a boy with a red birthmark between his brows who called himself the Embroidered Tiger. It was [Cui Chan](Cui_Chan.md). Zhao Yao gave up the spring seal in exchange for a secret cultivation technique and two immortal tools, and those gifts carried him through every danger on the road.<sup>[[8]](#ref-8)</sup> Qi Jingchun had foreseen this and told him not to worry about the seal. Chen Ping'an later realised that his teacher had probably wanted Zhao Yao to lose the seal so that he would be free of any ties to him.<sup>[[9]](#ref-9)</sup>

When he finally reached the foot of the sect's mountain, Zhao Yao changed his mind. He untied the ox, set it free and headed west to the sea alone. From there he took ship after ship toward the Middle Earth Divine Continent.

### The island

More than halfway into the voyage, a swarm of flying fish smashed his ship apart. Zhao Yao survived on a plank of wreckage until he passed out. He woke on a small island in a thatched cottage piled with books, cared for by a quiet man in azure robes. A rusted sword stood driven into the island's peak. In his despair Zhao Yao walked to the edge of a cliff meaning to jump, and the man stopped him with a few gentle words.<sup>[[10]](#ref-10)</sup>

He stayed on the island for nearly two years, reading and staring out to sea, until his spirits recovered. Before leaving on a raft he had built himself, he tried to give the man his wooden dragon paperweight as thanks. The man refused it, remarking that the world could not be too bad yet.

Zhao Yao never learned the rest. A remnant of Qi Jingchun's soul had guided him to the island and asked its master, a scholar who had once split the Yellow River Small World with a single sword strike, to save him. Qi Jingchun chose not to show himself, believing the boy would only feel guiltier. He had hidden his own kindred character inside the wooden dragon paperweight, waiting for the day Zhao Yao's heart found a new spring. He said that whether Zhao Yao ever inherited his lineage mattered far less than letting him seek knowledge in peace.<sup>[[11]](#ref-11)</sup>

### Later

Qi Jingchun left a wisp of spring wind on three of the town's children, Chen Ping'an, Song Jixin and Zhao Yao. He also revealed that Zhao Yao found another fated opportunity on his journey to another continent.<sup>[[12]](#ref-12)</sup> Of his two students from the small town, Qi Jingchun actually held Zhao Yao in somewhat higher regard than Song Jixin.

## Trivia

- The wooden dragon carving his grandmother gave him is counted among the great fated treasures to come out of the Jewel Small World, alongside Gao Xuan's golden carp and Ruan Xiu's fire dragon bracelet.
- An old man from the Zhao Clan who still lives in the small town is the ancestor of Zhao Yao's branch of the family.
- At the private school's Go table, Song Jixin always sat on the north seat and Zhao Yao always sat on the south.
- Cui Dongshan once told Song Jixin that his disposition was slightly better than Zhao Yao's.<sup>[[13]](#ref-13)</sup>
