## Appearance

Zhong Qiu is a thin, serious-looking man with a scholarly demeanor, dressed in an azure robe. His plain look belies his status, so much so that it was hard for those who first met him to believe he was the leading figure among the world’s four grandmasters. *(Chapter 307)*

## Personality

He carries himself with the discipline of both a scholar and a martial artist, insisting that every word he speaks and every punch he throws adhere strictly to principle. *(Chapter 315)* He is blunt and not given to idle talk, but he is also fair-minded, candid, and willing to hold back in a fight rather than strike an opponent who is unprepared. *(Chapter 315)* As a teacher he is strict and protective of his student’s foundation, growing visibly angry when he feels that foundation is being undermined. *(Chapter 388)* Once freed from his old responsibilities he becomes noticeably more relaxed, unashamed to borrow money or enjoy small pleasures he once denied himself. *(Chapter 603)*

## Background

Zhong Qiu and Yu Zhenyi came from humble backgrounds in Pine Song Nation and grew up as neighbors and inseparable friends, traveling the world together and each obtaining their own fated opportunity that elevated them into renowned martial arts prodigies. The two eventually turned on each other and fought a mortal duel witnessed by only a handful of people, leaving both of them severely wounded. Afterward Zhong Qiu left for Southern Garden Nation and the two cut all ties, though the rivalry and mutual respect between them never fully faded. In Southern Garden Nation he rose to become Imperial Preceptor and was regarded as the strongest martial artist in the world, ranked among the four grandmasters of the Lotus Flower Blessed Land alongside Yu Zhenyi, Tong Qingqing, and Lu Fang. *(Chapter 307)*

When Old Demon Ding Ying revealed to him the existence of a world beyond their own, called Parasol Leaf Continent, and the possibility of ascending from the blessed land by killing or surviving other grandmasters, Zhong Qiu was shaken but kept his composure, pressing Ding Ying for his true intentions rather than reacting rashly. *(Chapter 314)*

During the chaos that followed, Zhong Qiu stepped in to save Lu Fang from a killing blow by Chen Ping’an, then challenged Chen Ping’an to a fight himself, deliberately holding back at first to test him. He created his own signature fist stance, called Mountain Peak, trained over many years to make him as immovable as a mountain, and was stunned when Chen Ping’an managed to grasp the essence of the stance after only a few exchanges. The two eventually traded a single decisive blow each, and Zhong Qiu was sent crashing backward and plowing a trench into the ground for dozens of meters. Though he only suffered minor injuries, it was the first time he had been defeated within an arm’s length, and from that day on he could no longer be called the strongest martial artist in that world. *(Chapter 315)* He was soon after named to the renewed list of Upper Ten Elites alongside Chen Ping’an and several other new faces. *(Chapter 316)*

Zhong Qiu later became a teacher to Cao Qinglang, a boy from the capital whom Chen Ping’an had once looked after, sharing the role informally with Lu Tai. He confronted Lu Tai directly after learning that Lu Tai had been feeding the boy a flood of miscellaneous knowledge beyond his years, insisting that a Confucian education built on solid classical foundations would serve him far better in the long run. *(Chapter 388)* Despite this friction, the two men respected each other and worked well together raising Cao Qinglang between them, Zhong Qiu grounding him in rigorous scholarship while Lu Tai broadened his worldview. He also personally guided the boy’s preparation for the imperial examination and gave him access to his own rare book collection. *(Chapter 483)* Zhong Qiu came to think of Cao Qinglang as something close to a true disciple, and expressed hope that the boy would one day speak up for ordinary people in a world that too often treated them as disposable pieces on a game board. *(Chapter 530)*

When the old Daoist priest who secretly oversaw the Lotus Flower Blessed Land split it in two, with part of it becoming Downtrodden Mountain’s Lotus Root Blessed Land, Zhong Qiu resigned as imperial preceptor once the Great Dao’s hold over the land was lifted, finding himself a much freer and lighter person for it. In the years that followed he broke through to the Vajra Body Tier, and later made a further breakthrough after a stroke of fortune. He accepted a position as a guest elder of Downtrodden Mountain and set out with Cao Qinglang to see the wider world for the first time in his life, traveling across the Lotus Root Blessed Land before continuing on toward the Majestic World. *(Chapter 567)* *(Chapter 569)*

Alongside Cao Qinglang and Pei Qian, Zhong Qiu traveled onward to the Sword Qi Great Wall and then into the Majestic World proper, visiting Old Dragon City, Stalactite Mountain, and the Ning Manor in the company of Chen Ping’an and Cui Dongshan. Having spent nearly his entire life confined to the capital of Southern Garden Nation, he was humbled by how much larger the wider world was than anything he had known, and he continued to guide Cao Qinglang’s scholarship even as he admitted that true cultivation guidance was beyond his own expertise. *(Chapter 603)* *(Chapter 604)* He went on to accompany Cao Qinglang further into the Majestic World, with Chen Ping’an entrusting them to continue their travels together through the Southern Whirl Continent. *(Chapter 612)*

## Trivia

- He created his own fist stance, Mountain Peak, built around training the back, shoulders, and elbows into a single immovable form. It took most of his own disciples decades to even approximate its outward form. *(Chapter 315)*
- Chen Ping’an later incorporated elements of Zhong Qiu’s teachings, including the Examining the Dragon method and the Mountain Peak stance, into his own fighting style. *(Chapter 315)*
- He once tried and failed to destroy an immortal item, the Five Mountains True Appearance Picture Scroll, out of concern that Yu Zhenyi would seize it, before eventually entrusting it to Cao Qinglang instead. *(Chapter 530)*
