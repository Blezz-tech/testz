# Cultivation

[← Wiki Index](../INDEX.md) · [Original Fandom Page](https://sword-of-coming.fandom.com/wiki/Cultivation)

---
**1. General Concepts**

- **Cultivation** is an overarching term that includes multiple paths such as Qi refiners, martial artists, and swordsmen.
- Cultivation is described as defying the Heavenly Dao, with cultivators constantly forging ahead against the flow of natural order.
- It is a long path that requires fortune, talent, aptitude, fated opportunities, and a mature temperament.
- There are also cultivators who rely on the bestowal of the heavens, where fortune and fate are incredibly important, and everything they do seems like an act of cultivation.
- Cultivation is a process that involves the refinement of the body, the development of one's souls and senses, and the strengthening of courage.
- A key aspect of cultivation is the accumulation and management of *qi*, which is as essential to a person as incense is to the Buddha.
- There are various methods for recording cultivation techniques, including carving characters on jade or bamboo, and teaching through feeling and understanding rather than words.

**2. Paths of Cultivation**

- **Qi Refiners**
  - Rely on the bestowal of the heavens, making fortune and fate essential.
  - They refine *qi* and combine both nurturing and refining together.
  - They can nurture a myriad of different *sword qi*, some profound and some shallow.
  - Their goal is to absorb as much spiritual energy as possible, though it is limited by the number of acupoints developed.
  - The lower five tiers of Qi refinement focus on cultivating one's flesh and include the Copper Skin Tier, the Grass Root Tier, the Willow Tendon Tier, the Bone Qi Tier, and the Furnace Molding Tier.
  - The first four of these tiers are focused on developing a tough physique.
  - The final tier, the Furnace Molding Tier, is a stage where one integrates everything together, and their body becomes a great tool for refining Qi.
  - The Middle Five Tiers for Qi refiners include the Cave Abode Tier, the Sea Observation Tier, the Dragon Gate Tier, the Golden Core Tier, and the Nascent Tier.
  - Upon reaching the Golden Core Tier, one can transform their entire sea of Qi into a golden core.
  - The Cave Abode Tier is a difficult hurdle.
  - Some Qi refiners can observe the world through their palms.
  - Some Qi refiners may be "handicapped" in certain small worlds.
- **Martial Artists**
  - Must take things step by step with no shortcuts.
  - Focus on the refinement of the body, with the first three tiers being relatively concrete and the following three being more ambiguous and involving the development of souls, senses, and courage.
  - The first three tiers are the Physique Refining Tier, which includes the Clay Embryo Tier, a foundational stage where one is still rough and unrefined.
  - The Wooden Fetus Tier depends solely on one's ability to endure hardship.
  - The seventh tier is the Vajra Body Tier, where one attains an indestructible body.
  - The eighth tier is the Wing Formation Tier, where martial artists can suspend themselves in the air and fly.
  - The ninth tier is the Mountain Summit Tier.
  - The tenth tier is know as the End Tier as there is no know path to he Upper Five Tiers as a Martial Artist.
  - They have a different approach to *qi* refinement, taking the opposite route compared with cultivators.
  - They do not rely on external treasures.
  - They focus on the circulation of True Qi, and must learn which acupoints the True Qi has to travel through and at what speed.
- **Swordsmen**
  - Focus on two matters: practicing the sword and refining the sword.
  - Practicing the sword involves learning sword techniques, while refining the sword involves tempering one's sword and bonded flying sword.
  - Refining a physical flying sword or nurturing a bonded flying sword requires precious resources.
- **Militarians**
  - Strive for an "unmatched aura".
  - They can be cultivators, Qi refiners, martial artists, and swordsmen.
  - They are known for being unkillable and for killing others with ease.
  - They are completely uncaring and without qualms, daring to kill anyone that they please.

**3. Tiers of Cultivation**

- **General Tiers**
  - All martial arts are divided into nine tiers, though some say there is a 10th tier.
  - Cultivators also have tiers, though they have different names and requirements than martial arts tiers.
  - The Lower Five Tiers are generally concerned with the refinement of the body.
  - The Middle Five Tiers involve the development of the soul and the ability to manifest heart strings.
  - The Upper Five Tiers are a lofty goal for most cultivators.
  - Reaching the 10th tier is a significant achievement.
- **Martial Arts Tiers**
- The first three tiers involve refinement of the body.
- The Clay Embryo Tier is the first tier of the Physique Refining Tier, where one is still rough and unrefined.
- The Wooden Fetus Tier depends solely on one's ability to endure hardship.
- The seventh tier is the Vajra Body Tier.
- The eighth tier is the Wing Formation Tier.
- The ninth and final tier is the Mountain Summit Tier.
- **Qi Refinement Tiers**
  - The Lower Five Tiers include the Copper Skin Tier, the Grass Root Tier, the Willow Tendon Tier, the Bone Qi Tier, and the Furnace Molding Tier.
  - The final tier of the Lower Five Tiers is the Furnace Molding Tier.
  - The Middle Five Tiers include the Cave Abode Tier, the Sea Observation Tier, the Dragon Gate Tier, the Golden Core Tier, and the Nascent Tier.
  - The Dragon Gate Tier is a highly sought-after achievement.
  - The Unpolished Jade Tier is a high level of achievement.

**4. Methods and Techniques**

- **Acupoints:** Cultivation involves the use of acupoints for qi refinement.
- **Talismans:** Can be used in cultivation, though they are relatively rare in Buddhism.
- **Mystical Abilities:** Are developed over time and honed meticulously.
- **Soil Molding Technique:** A mystical technique.
- **Heart Strings:** Can be manifested in the Middle Five Tiers, with the number and thickness depending on the level of cultivation.
- **Sword Techniques:** Involve practice and refinement.
- **Qi Dispersion and Development:** Some families have unique cultivation methods for qi dispersion and development.

**5. External Factors and Influences**

- **Fortune and Fate:** Play an important role in cultivation, especially for Qi refiners.
- **Fated Opportunities:** Can lead to rapid progress in cultivation.
- **Environment:** Blessed lands are beneficial for cultivation.
- **Spiritual Energy:** The amount of spiritual energy one can absorb is dependent on the number of acupoints they've developed.
- **Wealth:** Can be used to enhance cultivation, with wealthy cultivators able to use spiritual fruits and other tools to cultivate.
- **Incense Offerings:** Can nurture spiritual nature and divine aura.
- **Inner State:** A cultivator's mental state plays a vital role in their cultivation and can be the determining factor in battles between cultivators.

**6. Challenges and Obstacles**

- **Bottlenecks:** Can be difficult to overcome, such as when progressing from the Lower Five Tiers to the sixth tier.
- **Flaws:** Knots in the heart can manifest as flaws in one's cultivation.
- **Heretical Paths:** There is the risk of straying down the wrong path or falling to a heretical path.
- **Qi Deviation:** A risk that can occur if one tries to follow a method that is too advanced for their current level.
- **External Pressures:** Cultivators in certain places may experience more pressure than others due to the nature of their location.
- **Loss of Progress:**  Cultivators can lose their progress due to various factors.

**7. Other Considerations**

- Some cultivators view physical refinement as less important than Qi refinement.
- Some cultivators believe that it is better to refine a small amount of Qi in a strong body than a large amount of Qi in a weak body.
- There is a hierarchy within cultivation, with different tiers and ranks.
- There are many ways to practice cultivation, some dull and requiring hard work, others more reliant on fated opportunities.
- Some cultivators, especially those from the mountains, view martial artists as crude and unrefined.
- Some cultivation practices may be considered taboo, for example the abilities of the School of Naturalists that involve gleaning the fortune and karma of others.
- Some techniques involve "inviting a god".
- Cultivators from the mountains can often observe the world through their palms, which can make them seem all knowing.
- Some cultivators become detached and apathetic.
- Cultivation may involve going against one's true nature.
- There is a concept of "accumulation before gradual release" in some forms of cultivation.
