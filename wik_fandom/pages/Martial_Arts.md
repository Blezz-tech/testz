# Martial Arts

[← Wiki Index](../INDEX.md) · [Original Fandom Page](https://sword-of-coming.fandom.com/wiki/Martial_Arts)

---
Martial arts, as presented in the sources, is a distinct path of cultivation focused on physical and spiritual development through combat techniques and rigorous training. It differs significantly from Qi refinement, though there are some overlaps, especially in the early stages of training. 

**Core Principles of Martial Arts**

- **Body as a Tool:** Martial artists view the body not merely as a vessel for Qi, but as a powerful tool to be honed and perfected.
- **Physical Prowess**: The focus is on developing physical strength, speed, agility, and endurance through specific training regimens.
- **Qi as Power**: Martial artists cultivate and manipulate their internal energy (True Qi) to enhance their physical abilities and combat techniques.
- **Meridians and Acupoints**: Martial artists focus on specific points in the body to channel energy through meridians, expanding and smoothing them.
- **Combat Orientation**: The primary focus is on combat, self-defense, and the ability to defeat opponents in battle.
- **Step by Step Progress:** Martial artists progress slowly and steadily through rigorous training, not by relying on luck or fated opportunities.
- **Inner Strength**: Though often seen as crude, martial artists seek to develop a strong inner self through discipline and endurance.
- **Self-Reliance**: Unlike Qi refiners, martial artists emphasize self-reliance, refining their souls and bodies by their own power.
- **Seeking the Extreme**: Some martial artists pursue an extreme path of breaking the old to establish the new, destroying acupoints to forge a new path.
- **Dao of the Fist**: Some martial artists, particularly those at higher levels, seek to elevate their martial arts to the level of Martial Dao.

**Tiers of Martial Arts**

- Martial arts is divided into nine tiers, with some claiming a **tenth tier** exists.
** Above the nine tiers, there is a legendary 10th tier where one becomes the peak of the mountain.
- The nine tiers are further grouped into three major tiers: **Physique Refining, Qi Refining, and Spirit Refining**.
- **Physique Refining Tier**
** Focuses on physical development and is further divided into three tiers:
  - **Clay Embryo Tier**: This initial tier is about laying the foundation, where the body is rough and unrefined. Reaching the peak of this tier results in a strong foundation where one's Qi will descend to the Dantian, and the practitioner becomes immovable.
  - **Wooden Fetus Tier**: This tier focuses on refining the physique, expanding meridians, and improving the pathways for Qi flow. This tier is also called the Mountain Carving Tier because of the hard work that must be invested to open the meridians.
  - **Mercury Tier**: At the peak of the third tier, a martial artist must undergo a tribulation, sometimes referred to as the "clay Bodhisattva crossing the river". The blood in one's body will become like mercury.

**Qi Refining Tier**

- Involves more than just bodily refinement, and also includes refining Qi. The tiers within this are focused on the souls, the spirit, and the gall. This tier is associated with the three immortal souls.
- It is believed that those at the sixth tier can begin to nurture their soul.
- Martial artists in the Three Qi Tempering Tiers will be elevated to encompass Qi refinement as well.

**Spirit Refining Tier**

- This tier is associated with the later stages of martial arts training.
- The seventh tier is the **Vajra Body Tier**, where a martial artist attains a body like a Buddhist vajra or a Daoist glazed body. At this point, one is considered a Lesser Grandmaster.
- The eighth tier is the **Wing Formation Tier**, or the Far Roaming Tier, where martial artists can fly.
- The ninth tier is the **Mountain Summit Tier**, which is the ultimate endpoint of the path of martial arts. Those at this level are referred to as Ultimate Grandmasters.

**Training Methods**

- **Standing, Walking, and Sleeping Meditations**: These practices strengthen the body and improve the flow of Qi. Sleeping meditation focuses on breath and accumulation within acupoints.
- **Fist Techniques**: Martial artists practice various fist techniques, and some focus on sword techniques as well.
- **Sparring**: Sparring is a way for martial artists to test and refine their skills. Sparring can be between master martial artists, or between martial artists and Qi refiners.
- **Hardship**: Enduring hardship is essential for progress, especially in the Wooden Fetus Tier.
- **Combat**: The process of breaking through is aided by combat, where martial artists transform the energy within their acupoints.
- **Tempering the Body**: The body is tempered with medicinal baths and other methods.

**Examining the Dragon**: One must understand how the spine allows one to generate explosive power and utilize True Qi.

**Drawing Talismans**: Drawing talismans can be a method to temper one's physique and train the soul.

**Sword Techniques**: Some martial artists also practice sword techniques, which are often considered more graceful than fist techniques.

**Finding the Qi**: Martial artists need to find the initial burst of qi within their bodies.

**Acupoint location**: A martial artist must find the most suitable acupoint for their qi to reside in.

**Key Concepts**

- **True Qi**: This is the internal energy that martial artists cultivate and use.
- **Martial Dao**: The ultimate goal for martial artists is to achieve the Martial Dao, which is a profound understanding of their art.
- **Fist Intent**: Martial artists use their fist intent to reach higher levels of understanding and power.
- **Soul, Spirit, and Gall**: These aspects are developed in the middle tiers and become interconnected at the seventh tier.
- **Heroic Spirit**: Militarian cultivators can nurture a heroic spirit that doesn't disperse after death.
- **Mental State**: The mental state of a martial artist is important, particularly when advancing to the fourth tier.

**Militarian Path:** This path is considered a shortcut, and it is dangerous because martial artists may fall into demonic ways.

**Cumulative Training**: Training is a cumulative process, and it subtly tempers one's mind and heart.

**No Second in Fist Techniques**: This saying relates to the idea that martial artists do not rely on external items, and so the winner is easy to guess.

**Challenges and Limitations**

- **Limited Lifespan:** Compared to Qi refiners, martial artists have a shorter lifespan.
- **Slower Progress**: Martial arts training is seen as more step-by-step and dull compared to the fated opportunities that often drive Qi refinement.
- **Physical Limitations**: The focus on the physical body is sometimes seen as a limitation, making it difficult to reach the same heights as Qi refiners.
- **Difficult Breakthroughs**: Advancing between tiers is difficult, especially from the third to the fourth tier, which is likened to a "clay deity crossing the river".
- **Risk of Injury**: Martial arts training can be damaging to the body if not done correctly.
- **Demonic Ways:** There is always the risk of a martial artist falling to demonic ways.
- **External Interference**:  Martial artists who rely too much on their physical power and disregard their souls and minds may become unstable.

**Limited Scope**: Some believe that martial arts is unrelated to the concept of Dao.

**Hardships**: The path of a martial artist involves a great deal of hardship, and there are no shortcuts.

**End Tier:** There are very few people who reach the 10th tier of martial arts.

**Competition**: There is competition between martial artists of the same tier, and those at lower levels will have difficulty competing with those at higher tiers.

**Differences from Qi Refinement**

- **Focus**: Martial arts emphasizes the body, while Qi refinement focuses on the spirit.
- **Progression**: Martial artists progress through hard work, while Qi refiners depend on fated opportunities and the bestowal of the heavens.
- **External Items**: Qi refiners use external items to aid in their cultivation, while martial artists tend to eschew such items.

**Qi**: Martial artists and Qi refiners take different routes when it comes to Qi refinement.

**Body as Vessel vs. Tool**: Qi refiners treat the body as a vessel, whereas martial artists view the body as a tool.

**Lower Tiers:**  Qi refiners in the lower tiers focus on refining their body, which is similar to martial arts training.

**Unique Aspects**

- **Swordsmanship**: Some martial artists use swords, and swordsmanship is considered a graceful fighting style.
- **Militarian Cultivators**:  These martial artists use combat experience to enhance their cultivation.

**Heroic Spirit**:  Some martial artists can cultivate a heroic spirit.

**Feng Shui**:  Practicing martial arts is likened to a feng shui master searching for acupoints.

**Deity Drumming Technique:** Intense training sessions can be a way to temper one's mind and heart.

Martial arts is a demanding path of self-improvement, emphasizing physical prowess, rigorous training, and the cultivation of inner strength through combat. Although it is seen as limited in some ways compared to Qi refinement, the sources present martial arts as a respectable and powerful path in its own right, especially for those who value self-reliance and a step-by-step approach to progress.
