## Appearance

Chen Ping'an's mother is described as beautiful. A close friend of hers, [Gu Can](Gu_Can.md)'s mother, recalls that she was a bubbly, outgoing woman before her illness. Within less than a year of falling ill, she was reduced to "nothing more than a sack of skin and bones", so emaciated that her neighbours were frightened to see her. *(Chapter 17)*

## Personality

She is remembered as a gentle, warm-hearted woman with "a fantastic personality". Gu Can's mother cannot recall a single time she ever became angry at anyone. She and her husband were generous and kind, which is why their neighbours in [Clay Vase Alley](Small_Town.md#clay-vase-alley) helped their orphaned son after their deaths. *(Chapter 189 (1))*

She was also self-sacrificing to the end. Throughout her illness, she told her son that it didn't hurt and only made her drowsy, so that he would worry less and do less for her. Chen Ping'an only realised much later that she had been gritting her teeth through terrible agony. *(Chapter 483 (1))* Even in her final days, she felt blessed to have such a good child, and murmured that she could not believe her luck that he was her son.

## Background

Chen Ping'an's mother grew up in the small town and married a quiet, awkward potter. In Gu Can's mother's opinion, he was not good enough for her. Their family of three lived a stable, happy life until her husband died young, either by slipping into the creek while rushing to the kilns on a rainy day or by trespassing onto a sealed mountain, depending on the rumour.

One of Chen Ping'an's few clear childhood memories is of sitting on his mother's lap while she mended clothes. His father asked her, in what was perhaps the only romantic thing he ever said, whether they would be together again in their next lives, and she smiled and answered, "Why not?" *(Chapter 218 (2))* She once ate a tartary buckwheat cake, told her son it was delicious, and promised to buy him one for his fifth birthday. Years later, Chen Ping'an tries to buy one to offer at his parents' graves, only to find that the pastry shop no longer makes them. *(Chapter 85 (1))*

After her husband's death, she fell gravely ill. Her young son looked after her alone, standing on a stool to reach the stove, buying and brewing her medicine, and searching the mountains for medicinal herbs to save money.

In the winter she died, her son came home from praying at a temple and asked whether she felt better. She smiled with great difficulty and said it no longer hurt at all, and the boy rejoiced that praying to the Bodhisattva worked. Before she died, it began to snow, and she sent him outside to look at it. Once he had gone, she closed her eyes and prayed with all her might: "Please, please, please, let my little Ping'an be blessed with safety and prosperity." She passed away before the turn of the year. *(Chapter 50 (2))*

Chen Ping'an was left an orphan and endured the greatest hardships imaginable between the ages of five and seven. He cut down his parents' old clothes to fit himself and carefully saved every offcut. *(Chapter 202)*

## Trivia

- Chen Ping'an once said that if he ever found a girl as good as his mother, he would fight to protect her even if his opponent were the Dao Ancestor. *(Chapter 203)*
- Watching her waste away left Chen Ping'an with a lasting fear of illness, and an even greater fear that if he died, no one in the world would remember his parents.
- Years later, the elderly Aunty Chen tearfully tells Chen Ping'an how wonderful it would have been for his mother to see his success, since she had known only hardship all her life. *(Chapter 466 (1))*
- Song Jixin once tricked Chen Ping'an into revoking a vow he had sworn to his mother.
- "Ping'an" (平安) means peace and safety, the very blessing she prayed for her son in her final moments.
