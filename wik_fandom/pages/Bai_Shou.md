## Appearance

As an assassin of Deer Cutter Mountain, Bai Shou dressed in a black robe and wore a white mask to conceal his identity, a disguise he cast aside the moment his cover was blown. *(Chapter 536)*

## Personality

He is brash, boastful, and quick to talk himself up, declaring within moments of meeting Chen Ping’an that he would become a renowned sword immortal within a hundred years. *(Chapter 536)* Underneath the bluster he is fiercely loyal to the one master he considers his true master, the mountain lord who raised him, and he refuses to recognize Qi Jinglong as a real replacement even while grudgingly obeying him. *(Chapter 539)* He has a habit of complaining about nearly everything, from cramped lodgings to his master’s moralizing, yet he quietly keeps a sharp assassin’s vigilance running under his lazy, bored exterior whenever there might be real danger nearby. *(Chapter 596)* He is not actually interested in chasing women despite his claims, simply enjoying being liked. *(Chapter 596)*

## Background

Bai Shou grew up at Deer Cutter Mountain, a faceless order of assassins with no fixed base and a strict code of rules, trained from a young age at the side of its mountain master and treated as a promising prospect because of his natural aptitude with the sword. *(Chapter 536)* When his master was sent to kill Chen Ping’an and failed to break through Qi Jinglong’s defenses after being allowed two free strikes, an agreement was struck that Deer Cutter Mountain would send its young prodigy to make one final, face-saving assassination attempt. Bai Shou was that prodigy, and when Chen Ping’an caught him lying in wait in a reed thicket, his attempt failed, binding him by the agreement to become Qi Jinglong’s disciple and leave Deer Cutter Mountain behind for good. *(Chapter 536)*

He resented the arrangement bitterly at first, mocking Qi Jinglong’s lectures and insisting he would only ever recognize his old master from Deer Cutter Mountain, even as he grudgingly accepted that his old master had likely engineered the whole situation for his own benefit. *(Chapter 539)* He grew to respect his new life more over time, becoming an official disciple of the Great Emblem Sword Sect and receiving a small cottage of his own on the sect grounds, though he still complained that it was far less luxurious than he had expected of a renowned sword immortal’s residence. *(Chapter 549)*

Bai Shou traveled with Qi Jinglong to Stalactite Mountain aboard Osmanthus Island, posing as a scholar’s attendant while secretly keeping watch for danger out of old habit. During the trip, Qi Jinglong arranged to request a Sword Nurturing Gourd from the Spring Banner Inn as a disciple-recruiting gift for him, a treasure so rare that even many sword immortals never owned one. *(Chapter 595)* He also grew noticeably more composed in the presence of senior figures of the sect after an encounter with the ancestral hall’s disciplinary elder, Huang Tong, even if he remained prone to blurting out embarrassing truths about his friend Chen Ping’an’s drinking habits. *(Chapter 596)*

When Qi Jinglong emerged from seclusion to face three formidable challengers, Li Cai, Dong Zhu, and Bai Shang, Bai Shou welcomed Chen Ping’an to the Great Emblem Sword Sect in his master’s stead, since Qi Jinglong was occupied receiving a stream of guests and visitors. He delighted in recounting how Qi Jinglong had been forced to turn away most callers, teasing Chen Ping’an about a visit from his acquaintance Xu Xingjiu, who had brought homemade wine all the way from Cloudtop City. *(Chapter 562)*

## Trivia

- He has a rivalry of sorts with Pei Qian, Chen Ping’an’s disciple, after being thoroughly embarrassed by her in a sparring match on Downtrodden Mountain, something he has never quite lived down. *(Chapter 595)*
- He was unaware for some time that his master’s birth surname was Qi rather than Liu, picking up the truth only through rumors at the sect. *(Chapter 595)*
- Despite his complaints, he was given the chance to request a Sword Nurturing Gourd of his own, a gift few disciples of any sect could expect so early in their cultivation. *(Chapter 595)*
