# Small Town

[← Wiki Index](../INDEX.md) · [Original Fandom Page](https://sword-of-coming.fandom.com/wiki/Small_Town)

---
The Small Town is the settlement where [Chen Ping'an](Chen_Ping_An.md) was born and raised, located inside [Jewel Small World](Small_Worlds.md) before it fell to the ground and was annexed by the [Great Li Empire](Great_Li_Empire.md). The town now sits inside Locust Yellow County, the subordinate county carved out when the surrounding territory was elevated into [Dragon Spring Prefecture](Dragon_Spring_Prefecture.md). The town itself kept its old boundaries and its own long, hidden history even after the annexation.

## General Characteristics

The town has no walls around it. What people call the town gate is really just a stretch of makeshift fencing where visitors are checked before being let in or sent away.<sup>[[1]](#ref-1)</sup> Fewer than 700 clans live there, adding up to around 5,000 people once servants are counted, and the Lu, Li, Zhao, and Song clans are the most prominent among them. One resident describes the town as deceptive in its modesty, saying it "may only be a small town, but it's like a small puddle that appears to be innocuous from the outside, but actually holds unfathomably deep water."<sup>[[2]](#ref-2)</sup>

The town's recorded history stretches back 3,000 years, and it has carried several names over that time. It appears on old maps of the Great Li Empire as Great Marsh Village before a sage personally renamed it Dragon Den. The town was renamed again later, to avoid sharing a character with the reigning Great Li emperor's name, but the sentence revealing that name is cut off before it is given, so the town's name in the period just before annexation is not stated outright.<sup>[[3]](#ref-3)</sup>

Nearly every generation born in the town produces people of notable talent, and most of them are taken away from the town while still young. [Song Jixin](Song_Jixin.md), who has read through the town's local chronicle more than once, noticed that almost none of these children ever return to the town once they leave it.<sup>[[4]](#ref-4)</sup> Mystical abilities and cultivation are also suppressed inside the town, and the rule holds for residents and visitors alike: the stronger someone's cultivation, the more backlash they take on by using it there.<sup>[[5]](#ref-5)</sup>

## Key Locations within the Town

### Clay Vase Alley

Chen Ping'an's home sits on Clay Vase Alley, a cramped and narrow stretch that he grew up thinking of as ordinary until he saw how differently the wealthier clans lived. It is the poorest part of the Town.

### Apricot Blossom Alley

Apricot Blossom Alley holds the Iron Lock Well, where a thick metal chain hangs down into the water for no reason anyone in town can explain. One curious resident once tried to pull the whole chain out, and died of unexplained bleeding the same night, a fate only undone once his family carried his body back to the well and let the chain sink back down.

### Fortune Street and Peach Leaf Alley

Fortune Street and Peach Leaf Alley are where the town's wealthiest clans live, paved with bluestone slabs and lined with peach trees whose blossoms strike at least one visiting student as nothing like the peach blossoms described in his books.<sup>[[6]](#ref-6)</sup>

### Dragon Riding Alley

Dragon Riding Alley is home to a blacksmith's forge, as well as a pastry shop known for a peach blossom wine cake, and the general store Burclover next door where Chen Ping'an later buys ink and writing supplies.<sup>[[7]](#ref-7)</sup>

### Other landmarks

- At the edge of town stands the stone archway that locals call the Crab Archway, after the twelve stone pillars that hold it up, though Song Jixin insists its official name is the Grand Secretary Archway, bestowed by an emperor to honor a military official.<sup>[[8]](#ref-8)</sup> Nine of its twelve pillars are carved with dragons, and the remaining three carry the White Tiger, the Black Tortoise, and the Vermillion Bird.<sup>[[9]](#ref-9)</sup>
- The covered bridge crosses the creek on the town's south side. Beneath it lies a rusted iron sword three thousand years old, and the Great Li Empire spent a great deal of money building the bridge for the sole purpose of containing what remains of the sword's power.<sup>[[10]](#ref-10)</sup>
- The old locust tree is another fixture of the town, said to sway and rustle even when the air is completely still, and it has long served as a gathering spot where an old storyteller sets up to tell tales to the local children.
- The private school, funded jointly by the town's most affluent clans, was where [Qi Jingchun](Qi_Jingchun.md) taught for decades, limiting his lessons to just three foundational texts: "Principles of Life for Children," "Rites and Music," and "An Anthology of Literary Essays."<sup>[[11]](#ref-11)</sup>
- The office of the kiln supervision official, once the seat of authority over the town's dragon kilns, is occupied by [Song Changjing](Song_Changjing.md) during his time in the town.<sup>[[12]](#ref-12)</sup>

## Unique Aspects and Mysteries

No one in the town, however old, can explain who hung the chain in the Iron Lock Well or why. Song Jixin has taken to calling the town's local chronicle the "Sixty-Year Chronicle," since it is updated roughly every sixty years, and "Beyond the Wall," after noticing how the people it describes almost never return home once they leave the town.

The deeper mystery behind the town is its origin. Qi Jingchun tells Chen Ping'an that the town is the burial site of the last true dragon in the world, and that the clash which killed it, three thousand years ago, drew enough cultivators to the area that many of them stayed, married, and settled, eventually growing into the town that exists now. Afterward, four Sages, representing the Confucianist, Buddhist, Daoist, and martial artist traditions, agreed to take turns guarding the town in rotations of sixty years each.

Every child born in the town is given a secret code name, and a drop of their heart's blood is sealed into a piece of porcelain fired specially for them, six years for girls and nine for boys, in what the town calls [bonded porcelain](Natal_Porcelain.md). These pieces are later bought and sold by outside parties, and someone tampering with a bonded porcelain can upend the life of the person it belongs to, as happened to Chen Ping'an's own father.

## Social and Economic Aspects

Wealth in the town runs along clan lines, with the Lu, Li, Zhao, and Song clans at the top and their servants and poorer neighbors well below them. The town's economy was long built around its dragon kilns, with the Lu Clan alone owning more of them, and better ones, than any other clan. The imperial court eventually shut all of the kilns down, ending that trade in the town.

## The Town's Fate

Qi Jingchun describes the town as a 3,000-year-old piece of chinaware worn down to the point of shattering, and warns that if it finally breaks, the thousands of people living there will not simply die, but face what he calls "a great death, a death where there is no next life."<sup>[[13]](#ref-13)</sup> He goes on to call the town's nature "innately discordant with the Great Dao," the price the Sages agreed to pay in exchange for the town's three thousand years of fortune.

When that reckoning finally arrives, Qi Jingchun chooses to take the full weight of it onto himself rather than let the town's people suffer, declaring simply, "I will shoulder it all."<sup>[[14]](#ref-14)</sup> Had he not done so, every one of the town's more than 6,000 residents would have been made to answer for it, and [Ruan Qiong](Ruan_Qiong.md), set to take over as the next Sage, would have been bound by his role to see that none of them escaped that reckoning.<sup>[[15]](#ref-15)</sup>
