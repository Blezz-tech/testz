**Cui Chan** is the imperial preceptor of the Great Li Empire and the former first disciple of the [Scholarly Sage](Scholarly_Sage.md). He split his soul into two, and the younger half later became [Cui Dongshan](Cui_Dongshan.md). He is regarded as one of the finest Go players on Eastern Treasured Vial Continent and treats people and nations as pieces on a board.

## Appearance

The young body Cui Chan used in the small town was a handsome boy with a red birthmark between his brows. When he smiled, Chen Ping'an thought he looked like a harmless fox spirit.<sup>[[1]](#ref-1)</sup> His true self is an old man who looks no younger than the Scholarly Sage.<sup>[[2]](#ref-2)</sup> When the two halves met, the young one wore flowing white robes and the old one azure Confucian robes.<sup>[[3]](#ref-3)</sup>

## Personality

Cui Chan is brilliant, talkative and coldly calculating. Chen Ping'an found that only Liu Baqiao could match his chatter, and that he stayed completely at ease even around powerful cultivators. He likes to be in full control of a situation and plans many moves ahead. Even Old Man Yang admired how precisely he could predict events.<sup>[[4]](#ref-4)</sup> He values results over conventional morality, yet he speaks warmly of his early years with A'Liang and the old scholar's other disciples.<sup>[[5]](#ref-5)</sup>

He is proud and resents being reminded of his past. When his grandfather brought up old matters, he cut him off and dismissed them as stale.<sup>[[6]](#ref-6)</sup> As imperial preceptor he is calm and exacting. He advises the young emperor against chasing other continents and explains how a ruler should earn and spend the empire's wealth.<sup>[[7]](#ref-7)</sup>

## Background

Cui Chan studied under his grandfather, [Cui Cheng](Cui_Cheng.md), and was later cast out of the Cui Clan.<sup>[[8]](#ref-8)</sup> He left home at twenty and reached Middle Earth Divine Continent at twenty-four, then spent more than a century through many ups and downs. He became the Scholarly Sage's first disciple and reached the peak of the 12th Tier as a Qi refiner.<sup>[[9]](#ref-9)</sup> The old scholar later admitted that, busy with a battle he had to win, he never explained his teachings properly, and Cui Chan turned against him in anger and frustration.<sup>[[10]](#ref-10)</sup> He wandered the continents before becoming the imperial preceptor of the Great Li Empire. He held no court post there but led the empire's cultivators.

For the sake of his Great Dao, he placed half of his soul in a body shed by an ancient immortal. The old Cui Chan stayed in the capital while the young one entered the small town and lived at the Yuan Clan's ancestral residence.<sup>[[11]](#ref-11)</sup> Through Cui Minghuang he deceived [Ma Zhan](Ma_Zhan.md) into betraying Qi Jingchun, and he used the young Wu Yuan as a test of loyalty. After Qi Jingchun's death, his remaining soul confronted Cui Chan, who mocked him for having nothing left but a wisp of soul.<sup>[[12]](#ref-12)</sup>

The young half took the name Cui Dongshan and followed Chen Ping'an as a student. Before leaving the town, the old Cui Chan told Cui Minghuang that he now had three kinds of disciples in Cui Minghuang, Wu Yuan and the porcelain boy. The Scholarly Sage called the young Cui Chan and Chen Ping'an his half disciples, and said the real Cui Chan was the old man in the capital.

Cui Chan later visited his grandfather in the bamboo building on Downtrodden Mountain. When he met Cui Dongshan again, he offered a way to fuse their souls back together, but Cui Dongshan refused. He later made a bet with Cui Dongshan over whether Chen Ping'an could escape the trap that Bamboo Scroll Lake posed for him. The loser would have to accept the other as the primary self. He also spoke with Chen Ping'an at length about the dangers facing the world and why A'Liang had spared him in the Great Li capital.<sup>[[13]](#ref-13)</sup> Years later he returned to Downtrodden Mountain and quarrelled bitterly with Cui Dongshan over their grandfather's choices.<sup>[[14]](#ref-14)</sup>

## Trivia

- He once played Go among the clouds with the city lord of White Emperor City.
- He knew A'Liang longer than Qi Jingchun did, and he drank with A'Liang while Ma Zhan and Mao Xiaodong were still children.
- His given name means the sound of flowing water. His grandfather suggested a different character, also read as Chan, that means tall mountain.
- The Scholarly Sage believed that if Cui Chan held to his new path, he might still do the world much good.
