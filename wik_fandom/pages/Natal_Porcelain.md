# Natal Porcelain

[← Wiki Index](../INDEX.md) · [Original Fandom Page](https://sword-of-coming.fandom.com/wiki/Natal_Porcelain)

---
Natal Porcelain, also called "bonded porcelain", is the system through which the small town's most gifted children are identified, claimed, and controlled by outside cultivators and sects.

## Origins and Process

Every child born in the small town is given a secret code name recorded in a hidden document. At some point after birth, a specific drop of that child's heart's blood is extracted through a secret technique and sealed into a piece of porcelain fired for them in one of the town's dragon kilns. A girl's porcelain must be fired for six years, while a boy's must be fired for nine, with the kiln fire kept burning without a single day's interruption.<sup>[[1]](#ref-1)</sup>

Once a child reaches that age, six for girls and nine for boys, their fate is effectively decided. If an outside buyer has taken an interest in them by then, they are taken from the town to be raised and trained elsewhere. A boy who reaches nine without being claimed by a porcelain buyer is considered to have lackluster cultivation potential and is left in the town, considered to hold little further value.<sup>[[2]](#ref-2)</sup> The piece of porcelain belonging to a child judged to have no potential is removed from the kiln, shattered, and discarded on a mountain of broken porcelain outside the town built up from these failures. No one in the town seems to know what becomes of the children themselves once this happens, beyond assuming nothing good follows.<sup>[[3]](#ref-3)</sup>

## The Porcelain Buyers

A piece of natal porcelain is bought and sold like any other asset, and "porcelain buying rights" can change hands between outside sects and clans more than once over a person's life, sometimes through outright extortion between rival powers.<sup>[[4]](#ref-4)</sup> The clans inside the town that control the most dragon kilns are also the ones best positioned to supply natal porcelain to the outside world, and a buyer who ends up with a particularly gifted piece of porcelain will often reward the clan that sold it to them with a substantial additional payment on top of the original price.

Whoever holds a person's natal porcelain holds power over that person's life for as long as they live. If the porcelain's owner needs help with something that threatens their clan or sect's survival, the person bound to it is obligated to provide it, and refusing means having their porcelain smashed. This leash holds even for people who go on to become extraordinarily powerful; two such natives of the small town, the Daoist True Lord Cao Xi and the sword immortal [Xie Shi](Xie_Shi.md), are said to be strong enough that most porcelain buyers would rather treat them as honored guests than as owned property, yet neither can escape the arrangement entirely.

## Consequences and Fate

Whether or not someone sold as natal porcelain goes on to achieve great cultivation, the price for it is steep. Once such a person advances to the Middle Five Tiers of cultivation, they lose the right to any afterlife or reincarnation unless they can advance further still, to the Upper Five Tiers. Should they fail to do so, their soul is said to shatter and vanish entirely, beyond saving even by the Dao Ancestor or the Buddha.

Breaking or destroying a person's natal porcelain early severs this bond outright. It is described as a double-edged act: the person loses access to whatever fated opportunities the arrangement might have brought them, but is also freed from the porcelain buyers' control. [Chen Ping'an's father](Chen_Gong.md) discovered the truth behind natal porcelain after his own piece had already been tampered with by frustrated buyers who had written him off as worthless, and in response he secretly shattered his young son's porcelain, a porcelain paperweight, before Chen Ping'an turned six. [Liu Xianyang](Liu_Xianyang.md)'s bonded porcelain, by contrast, changed hands between rival outside powers, including one buyer who extorted it away from a rival, without ever being destroyed.
